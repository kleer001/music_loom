# Classic & Brostep Dubstep Song Technical Breakdowns

Technical analyses of defining tracks from the classic (2003–2008) and brostep (2008–2012) dubstep eras, documenting BPM, key, arrangement, bass design, and production techniques.

---

## Skrillex — "Scary Monsters and Nice Sprites"

**BPM:** 140 BPM  
**Key:** B♭ Major (though some sources list G Minor)  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Length:** ~4:03  

### Production Background
- Created in ~2 weeks at Skrillex's (Sonny Moore's) apartment on a laptop, using only one working speaker after the other blew out
- Originally a test for the Native Instruments FM8 synthesizer
- Recorded in 2010, released as the lead track on the *Scary Monsters and Nice Sprites* EP

### Sound Design & Synthesis
**Bass Design:**
- Wobble basslines created using **FM (frequency modulation) synthesis** via the Native Instruments **FM8** plugin
- FM8 synthesizer produces the signature "metallic, growling" timbres and aggressive wobbling bass sounds
- Heavy sub-bass drops provide low-end impact; bass drops around 60–80 Hz range

**Effects & Texture:**
- Glitchy effects created via automated filtering, distortion, and granular processing
- High-pitched melodic elements and "serpentine melody" contrasting the bass bombs
- Vocal sample: Rachael Nedrow ("speedstackinggirl") shouting "Yes, oh my gosh!"

### Arrangement & Structure
- Opens with melodic, high-pitched intro (~0:40) with pleasantly fragmented vocal samples
- Sharp transition into aggressive bass bombs at ~0:40–0:50 mark ("sound like alien invasion")
- Multiple bass drop sections with varying intensity
- Typical dubstep structure: intro → build → drop → breakdown → drop → outro

### Key Production Insight
The track exemplifies brostep's defining approach: combining melodic, almost pop-influenced top lines with brutally aggressive, heavily processed bass drops using FM synthesis. Moore's constraint (single working speaker) forced pure ear-critical mixing decisions without room-accurate monitoring.

## Skrillex — "Bangarang" (feat. Sirah)

**BPM:** 216 BPM (often felt as half-time 108 BPM)  
**Key:** G Minor  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  

### Production Approach
- Half-time feels paired with aggressive basslines create seismic rhythmic tension
- Bass design relies on **layering**: sub-bass combined with growling mid-range basslines for full-bodied, cutting bass

### Sound Design & Synthesis
**Bass Synthesis:**
- **FM synthesis** using modulator oscillator (sawtooth wave) and carrier oscillator (sine wave)
- Modulation index gradually increased to create harmonic complexity in the 100–300 Hz range
- Layered sub-bass with growling mids produces both clarity and depth

**Structure:**
- Half-time drum patterns (half the BPM feel) under full-speed synth elements
- Aggressive percussion punctuation throughout

### Key Production Insight
"Bangarang" demonstrates Skrillex's refinement of the FM-synthesis dubstep formula introduced in "Scary Monsters and Nice Sprites," focusing on bass layering and half-time rhythmic tension to create impact on dance floors.

## Skream — "Midnight Request Line"

**BPM:** 140 BPM  
**Key:** Not explicitly documented  
**DAW:** FL Studio (Fruity Loops)  
**Release Date:** December 2003 (written on Christmas Day 2003, when Skream was 17 years old)  

### Production Background
- Created in one session on FL Studio with minimal tools: "FL and probably four plugins made Midnight Request Line"
- Highest-selling release on Tempa Records; foundational to dubstep's global emergence
- Originally conceived as a grime track before finding its dubstep voice

### Sound Design & Synthesis
**Key Synths & Plugins:**
- **Primary synths:** Junglist (by Synapse Audio) and Absynth
- **Bassline:** TS404 (FL Studio's vintage analog synth emulation)
- Minimal plugin count forces deep mastery of each tool rather than plugin surfing

**Bass Design:**
- TS404 bassline created through unconventional workflow: no Piano Roll in early FL Studio, so Skream and collaborator Benga "drew in filters like velocity" through step sequencer automation
- This manual automation approach created "wilder" wobbles than standard LFO modulation
- Unusually high-pitched bassline for dubstep, providing melodic interest alongside the bass weight

### Arrangement & Structure
- Colorful, melodic approach with high-pitched synth elements
- Key change halfway through the track to add tension and interest
- Elements hang together organically despite simplicity; hard to imagine being pieced together from such basic tools

### Key Production Insight
"Midnight Request Line" exemplifies classical dubstep's aesthetic: space, restraint, and deep knowledge of a limited toolset over vast plugin collections. The workaround of using step sequencer velocity to create filter sweeps became a signature characteristic of early dubstep bass design.

## Benga & Coki — "Night"

**BPM:** 140 BPM  
**Key:** C♯ Minor / D♭ Minor  
**Camelot Key:** 12A  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Length:** ~2:53  
**Release:** 2008 on Tempa Records  

### Musical Context
- Pivotal classical dubstep release from the Tempa Records era
- Exemplifies the deep, gritty bass lines characteristic of London dubstep
- Known for its signature "wobbly bass sound"
- High energy and very danceable despite minimal arrangement

### Sound Design
**Wobble Bass:**
- Deep, reedy sub-bass around 40–80 Hz range
- Wobble created via LFO modulation of filter cutoff (classic dubstep approach)
- Warm, full-bodied bassline with minimal harmonic complexity

**Arrangement:**
- Minimal production: sparse, spacious drums layered under deep bass
- Vinyl crackle and ambient textures enhance the dark atmosphere
- Drum pattern uses skipped/syncopated hits typical of 2-step garage influence

### Key Production Insight
"Night" represents classical dubstep at its most refined: deep sub-bass with simple LFO-based wobble, minimal drums, and generous use of space. Benga and Coki avoided the melodic complexity that would later define brostep, focusing instead on bass weight and spatial arrangement.

## Flux Pavilion — "Bass Cannon"

**BPM:** 140 BPM  
**Key:** G♯ Minor / F♯ Minor (sources vary)  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Length:** ~5:10  
**Release:** 2011 (lead single for Circus One compilation)  
**DAW:** Cubase  

### Production Background
- Recorded by Joshua Kiekegaard Steele (Flux Pavilion) in 2010
- Lead single for Circus Records' debut compilation *Circus One*
- Established Flux Pavilion as a key figure in modern dubstep/bass music

### Sound Design & Synthesis
**Bass Design:**
- **Primary Synthesizer:** Native Instruments Massive
- Wobble bass created using Massive's flexible oscillator and modulation capabilities
- Heavy sub-bass lines with aggressive, dancefloor-oriented character
- Layered percussion built from breakbeat-inspired samples

**Sonic Characteristics:**
- Melancholic overall tone despite aggressive bass drops
- High energy and very danceable despite somber key choice (minor)
- Multiple drop sections with varying intensity and filter manipulation

### Key Production Insight
"Bass Cannon" refined the brostep formula by combining melancholic harmonic content with aggressive wobble bass synthesis in Massive, creating a track that works both emotionally and dancefloor-physically.

## Flux Pavilion — "I Can't Stop"

**BPM:** 140–143 BPM  
**Key:** C Minor  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Length:** ~5:06  
**Release:** 2010 on Circus Records / Big Beat  

### Musical Context
- Somber, introspective dubstep track despite aggressive production
- Sampled by Kanye West & Jay-Z on a major hip-hop production, elevating dubstep's cultural footprint
- Demonstrates dubstep's crossover appeal beyond club contexts

### Sound Design
**Bass & Harmonic Content:**
- C Minor key creates dark, minor-key mood throughout
- Heavy sub-bass drops with aggressive wobble synthesis
- Bass frequencies dominate 40–120 Hz range

### Arrangement & Structure
- High energy despite melancholic tone
- Multiple drop sections with varying intensity
- Percussion-focused buildups leading into bass-heavy drops

### Key Production Insight
"I Can't Stop" exemplifies brostep's emotional range: using minor keys and somber harmonic choices within an aggressive, heavy bass production framework. The track's cultural sampling (Kanye/Jay-Z) helped establish dubstep as a sampled source for mainstream hip-hop.

## Rusko — "Cockney Thug"

**BPM:** 140 BPM  
**Key:** C♯ Minor / D♭ Minor (F♯ Minor also cited)  
**Time Signature:** 4/4  
**Length:** ~4:09  
**Release:** 2008  

### Production Background
- Released on Sub Soldiers label in 2008
- Gained popularity with energetic melody and heavy drops
- Represents the playful, catchy side of classical dubstep

### Sound Design & Synthesis
**Bass Design:**
- Wobble bass with memorable, catchy melodic content
- Synthesis uses LFO modulation of sawtooth/square oscillators
- Heavy, punchy sub-bass drops (40–80 Hz)

**Wobble Bass Technique:**
- LFO rate synced to 140 BPM (quarter or eighth notes for pulse)
- Heavily processed with distortion, compression, and sidechain effects
- Reverb and delay add spatial depth

**Sonic Character:**
- Energetic and playful approach to dubstep (contrasts somber tracks)
- Catchy melodic hook within aggressive bass framework
- Heavy distortion on mid-range frequencies (400–2000 Hz)

### Arrangement & Structure
- Dynamic production with varying intensity across sections
- Multiple drop sections with memorable bass motifs
- Percussion uses breakbeat-inspired samples with heavy sidechain compression

### Key Production Insight
"Cockney Thug" demonstrates Rusko's signature approach: combining wobble bass aggression with melodic, hook-driven songwriting. Rusko pioneered the use of wobble bass as a melodic tool, not just textural effect, influencing Skrillex and the broader brostep movement.

## Nero — "Promises" (feat. Juliet Sikora)

**Original Track:**
- **BPM:** 72 BPM  
- **Key:** A Minor  

**Skrillex & Nero Remix:**
- **BPM:** 104 BPM (some sources: 72–140 BPM range depending on version)  
- **Key:** A Minor  
- **Genre:** Dubstep  

### Musical Context
- Original: soulful, R&B-influenced production
- Nero remix: transforms into dubstep while preserving vocal-centric arrangement
- Charted at #81 on Billboard Hot 100 (week ending July 14, 2012) — Nero's first US chart entry
- Skrillex & Nero Remix won Grammy Award for Best Remixed Non-Classical Recording (2013)

### Vocal Production
**Approach:**
- Vocals treated as a melodic tool woven into the production itself
- Topline vocal by Juliet Sikora allowed to "weave into the production" rather than sitting above
- Builds constructed to open space for larger vocal runs
- High-end synths and undulating basslines support vocal phrasing

### Sound Design
**Bass & Synthesis:**
- Searing, high-end synths contrast with undulating sub-bass
- Metronomic beat provides rhythmic foundation
- Vocal doubling and harmonies add melodic richness

**Arrangement:**
- Dubstep remix scales 72 BPM (original) to higher tempo range
- Orchestral string samples and lush harmonic content
- Rhythm section uses classic dubstep half-time feel with modern production polish

### Key Production Insight
"Promises" exemplifies Nero's approach: combining orchestral/classical music sensibilities with dubstep bass design. The Skrillex remix maintains vocal-as-instrument philosophy while introducing aggressive bass drops, creating a bridge between R&B/pop vocal production and dubstep's aggressive aesthetic.

**Note:** Nero also recorded *The Dubstep Symphony* with the BBC Philharmonic Orchestra (2011), demonstrating their interest in blending dubstep with orchestral arrangements.

## Burial — "Archangel"

**BPM:** 135 BPM (half-time feel at 68 BPM; can double to 270 BPM)  
**Key:** D♭ Major / C♯ Major (enharmonically equivalent)  
**Camelot Key:** 3B  
**Time Signature:** Variable (syncopated 2-step garage patterns)  
**Release:** November 5, 2007 (Track #2 on *Untrue*)  

### Production Background
- Recorded on ancient Sony Sound Forge (outdated version) running on a "rubbish, dying computer" that "occasionally leaked smoke"
- No sequencing capabilities; Burial arranged samples on a "blank and grid-less timeline"
- Visual-based workflow: arranged by looking at waveforms; "I know when I'm happy with my drums because they look like a nice fishbone"

### Vocal Sampling & Sound Design
**Vocal Processing:**
- Primary vocal sample: Ray J's "One Wish" — cut into individual pieces, then "reassembled while pitching each slice individually to create a new vocal melody"
- Secondary vocal: pitched-up "oh no" samples from Christina Aguilera's *Saturday Night Live* performance
- Both vocals layered over orchestral strings sampled from the *Metal Gear Solid* original soundtrack

**Vocal Arrangement:**
- Vocals function as atmospheric texture, not melodic lead
- Pitch-shifted, time-stretched, and heavily processed with reverb and echo
- Organic, human vocal timbres contrast with digital processing

### Drum Design
**Percussion & Rhythm:**
- Opens with "blocky, wooden" percussion sounds
- Couched in crackly ambient textures and vinyl crackle
- Syncopated 2-step garage-influenced beat patterns (drums skip and syncopate around the 4/4 grid)
- Dark, warm sub-bass providing foundation under sparse drums

**2-Step Garage Heritage:**
- Syncopated drum patterns reference UK garage and early jungle rhythmic conventions
- Skipped snares and hi-hats create "skittering" texture
- Clattering drums laid over dank sub frequencies

### Synthesis & Effects
**Harmonic Content:**
- Rising string samples from *Metal Gear Solid* provide harmonic lift
- Dark, minor-key atmosphere despite D♭ Major nominal key (likely due to sample pitch and context)
- Reverb-heavy production with "neon glow" contrast to "dank subs"

### Arrangement & Structure
- Minimal structure; tracks appear to fade in and out organically
- No traditional drops or buildups; instead uses textural density changes
- Approximately 4:00–4:30 duration (typical for Burial tracks)
- Vinyl crackle throughout creates aged, nostalgic aesthetic

### Key Production Insight
"Archangel" represents Burial's deconstruction approach: taking familiar vocal samples (R&B, pop) and orchestral recordings, then radically time-stretching, pitch-shifting, and processing them into unrecognizable textures. The "grid-less timeline" mixing approach in Sound Forge forced off-kilter rhythmic signatures that became Burial's defining characteristic. The track bridges UK garage's 2-step tradition with dubstep's sub-bass weight and modern electronic production, creating a sound that influenced both bass music and experimental electronic music.

## Production Technique Summary

### Common Elements Across Classical Dubstep (2003–2008)

- **Tempo:** 140 BPM standard (70 BPM half-time feel typical for mixing)
- **Key:** Minor keys predominant (G Minor, D♭ Minor, C Minor, A Minor)
- **Bass Design:** Deep sub-bass (40–80 Hz) with LFO-modulated wobble or FM-synthesis aggression
- **Synths:** Absynth, TS404, Junglist, Massive, FM8 (depending on era)
- **Arrangement:** Minimal, spacious; emphasis on bass and drums over melodic content
- **Effects:** Reverb, delay, vinyl crackle, distortion, sidechain compression
- **Philosophy:** Restraint and depth over melodic complexity

### Brostep Evolution (2008–2012)

- **Tempo:** Maintained 140 BPM but with half-time drum feels and faster synth elements
- **Key:** Still minor-dominated, but paired with melodic hooks and emotional vocal content
- **Bass Design:** FM synthesis (Skrillex, Bangarang) combined with aggressive wobble and layering
- **Harmonic Approach:** Added melodic and orchestral elements (Nero, "Promises")
- **Synthesis Tools:** FM8, Massive, Serum (as it emerged), Absynth
- **Arrangement:** Drops became more pronounced; buildups more melodic and emotional
- **Effects:** Heavier distortion, more aggressive sidechain, granular processing, bitcrushing
- **Philosophy:** Combining aggressive production with pop/melodic sensibilities

### Burial's Outlier Approach

- **Sampling-based:** Used found sounds, vocal snippets, and orchestral records rather than synthesis
- **Workflow:** Grid-less timeline arrangement forced unique rhythmic characteristics
- **Key Insight:** Pitch-shifting and time-stretching as primary "synthesis" tools
- **Aesthetic:** Atmospheric, nostalgic, bridging UK garage with experimental electronic music
