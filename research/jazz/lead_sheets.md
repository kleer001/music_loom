# Public-Domain Lead Sheets & Chord Changes — Reference Catalog

Reference material for the procedural-audio music engine: public-domain notation,
lead sheets, and chord-change skeletons for tunes that "jazz up" easily. The goal
is the **chord changes and melodic skeleton** (feedstock for a procedural
reharmonizer), not a recording.

## How to read public-domain status (US, as of 2026)

- **Rolling 95-year rule:** in the United States, published works enter the public
  domain 95 years after first publication. As of 2026, anything **first published
  before Jan 1, 1931** is PD in the US. (The cutoff advances one year each Jan 1.)
- **Composition vs. arrangement vs. recording are separate.** A PD melody can carry
  a *separately copyrighted* later arrangement, set of lyrics, or sound recording.
  We want the **underlying composition / changes**, which is what's PD here. Always
  source the *original early printing*, not a modern engraving, to be safe.
- **Traditional / folk tunes** have no single author and predate copyright, so the
  *core tune* is PD — but a specific published arrangement (e.g. a 20th-c. folio
  setting) can be under its own copyright. Flagged per tune below.
- **Jurisdiction:** statements here are **US-centric**. Several Handy tunes are PD
  in the US and life+50 countries but **NOT** in the EU / life+70 countries (Handy
  d. 1958, so EU PD in 2029). Flagged where relevant.

Chord changes below are the *standard / canonical* harmonic skeletons in common
circulation — confirm against the cited original notation before treating any
voicing as authoritative; reharmonizers should treat these as the diatonic floor.

## 1. Maple Leaf Rag — Scott Joplin (1899)

- **Composer / year:** Scott Joplin, pub. 1899 (John Stark & Son, Sedalia, MO).
- **PD rationale:** Published 1899 — PD in the US (pre-1931). Joplin d. 1917, so
  PD worldwide (life+70 expired). Clean, unambiguous.
- **Sources:**
  - IMSLP: https://imslp.org/wiki/Maple_Leaf_Rag_(Joplin,_Scott)
  - Mutopia (typeset, PD): https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/JoplinS/maple/maple-a4.pdf
  - Library of Congress (1899 first ed. scan): https://www.loc.gov/item/2023864251/
- **Key / form:** A-flat major (B section moves to D-flat). Classic multi-strain
  rag march form: **Intro – AA – BB – A – CC – DD** (each strain 16 bars, two-beat
  march feel, "Tempo di marcia"). Sectional, not AABA — each strain is its own
  16-bar period.
- **Changes (skeleton, A strain in Ab):** tonic-anchored ragtime cadences leaning on
  Ab – (Eb7) – Ab with chromatic passing diminished chords (e.g. Edim → Fm) and
  secondary dominants (C7 → Fm, F7 → Bb7 → Eb7). The C strain modulates to Db.
  Reharm value: dense secondary-dominant / diminished-passing motion already built
  in; easy to swing.

## 2. The Entertainer — Scott Joplin (1902)

- **Composer / year:** Scott Joplin, pub. 1902 (John Stark & Son, St. Louis).
- **PD rationale:** Published 1902 — PD in the US and worldwide (Joplin d. 1917).
  Wikimedia and LoC both mark it PD.
- **Sources:**
  - Wikisource (notation): https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Entertainer
  - Library of Congress: https://www.loc.gov/item/ihas.200033252/
  - IMSLP (under Joplin works) / Mutopia typesets available.
- **Key / form:** C major (with a section in F / a strain modulating). Rag form:
  **Intro – AA – BB – A – CC – DD**, 16-bar strains, "A rag time two step,"
  marked not too fast.
- **Changes (A strain in C):** C – G7 – C with ragtime ii–V and secondary-dominant
  motion (D7 → G7, A7 → Dm), chromatic walk-ups. C/trio strain shifts toward F.
  Reharm value: brighter and more lyrical than Maple Leaf; the melodic skeleton
  swings cleanly into a stride/jazz feel.

## 3. St. Louis Blues — W. C. Handy (1914)

- **Composer / year:** W. C. Handy, pub. September 1914 (Pace & Handy, Memphis).
- **PD rationale:** Published 1914 — **PD in the US** and life+50 countries.
  **FLAG:** Handy d. 1958, so it is **NOT PD in the EU / life+70 countries** until
  2029. US-only PD. Use the **1914 first edition** scan (later 1942/1963 editions
  carry their own arrangement copyrights).
- **Sources:**
  - IMSLP: https://imslp.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues_(Handy,_W._C.)
  - Wikimedia Commons (1914 Pace & Handy PDF):
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:W._C._Handy_-_St._Louis_Blues_(1914,_Pace_and_Handy).pdf
  - Wikisource: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues
  - Internet Archive scan: https://archive.org/details/thestlouisblues10249gut
- **Key / form:** **G major.** Hybrid multi-strain (ragtime-influenced), NOT a plain
  12-bar throughout:
  - **Strain 1 (verse):** standard **12-bar blues** in G.
  - **Strain 2 (the famous "tango"/habanera section):** **16 bars** in habanera
    rhythm, harmonically darker — leans on the relative/parallel minor color
    (the "Spanish tinge," per Jelly Roll Morton); built on a minor-tinged
    Gm-area progression over a habanera bass.
  - **Strain 3 (chorus):** returns to a **12-bar blues** in G.
- **Changes (12-bar blues in G):** G7 / G7 / G7 / G7 / C7 / C7 / G7 / G7 / D7 / C7 /
  G7 / D7. The habanera strain is the prize for a reharmonizer — minor-mode 16-bar
  contrast against the major-blues outer strains.

## 4. The Memphis Blues — W. C. Handy (1912)

- **Composer / year:** W. C. Handy, pub. September 1912 (self-published; subtitled
  "Mr. Crump"). Widely cited as the first commercially successful published blues.
- **PD rationale:** Published 1912 — **PD in the US** (pre-1931). Same EU/life+70
  caveat as St. Louis Blues (Handy d. 1958). **FLAG: US-PD; not EU-PD until 2029.**
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia (history/structure): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Memphis_Blues
  - Early-edition scans circulate via archive.org and LoC; verify the 1912/1913
    first printing for clean PD.
- **Key / form:** Multi-strain "southern rag." Historically notable for **mixing a
  16-bar melodic strain (familiar to audiences) with innovative 12-bar blues
  sections**, and pairing 2/4 march time with an Afro-Cuban habanera rhythm.
- **Changes:** the 12-bar sections follow standard I–IV–V blues (in the published
  key); the 16-bar strain is ragtime-cadential. Reharm value: documented blend of
  12- and 16-bar phrase lengths in one tune — useful for an engine that wants to
  vary phrase structure.

## 5. St. James Infirmary Blues — traditional / arr. "Joe Primrose" (Irving Mills), 1929

- **Origin / year:** Traditional ("Gambler's Blues"), descended from the 18th-c.
  British broadside "The Unfortunate Rake." First copyrighted arrangement
  registered **March 1929** under the pseudonym **"Joe Primrose"** (= publisher
  Irving Mills); Louis Armstrong recorded it Dec 1928, *before* the registration.
- **PD rationale — FLAG / UNCERTAIN:** The **underlying traditional tune/lyrics are
  PD** (predates copyright; circulated for years before 1929). HOWEVER, the
  specific **Mills/Primrose 1929 published arrangement** was claimed and enforced
  for decades. Treat the *traditional melody and changes* as PD, but **do NOT copy
  the Primrose engraving / arrangement** — derive from the traditional skeleton or a
  pre-1929 source. Net: safe as a *traditional tune*, risky as the *Primrose chart*.
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James_Infirmary_Blues
  - Old Time Blues (lyrics/history): https://oldtimeblues.net/lyrics/st-james-infirmary/
  - Copyright deep-dive (researcher Robert Harwood):
    https://iwentdowntostjamesinfirmary.blogspot.com/2014/04/copyright-and-st-james-infirmary.html
- **Key / form:** Commonly minor key; **8-bar minor blues / dirge** form (shorter
  than 12-bar). Often performed in C minor or D minor, slow 4/4.
- **Changes (8-bar minor, in C minor):** Cm / Cm – G7 / Cm – C7 / Fm / Cm – G7 /
  Cm. (Common variant adds Ab → G7 turnaround.) Reharm value: minor-mode dirge,
  ideal noir/funeral mood; thematically on-the-nose for a murder mystery.

## 6. House of the Rising Sun — traditional (English/Appalachian)

- **Origin / year:** American/English traditional folk ("Rising Sun Blues").
  Uncertain authorship; oldest published lyrics by Robert W. Gordon, 1925; first
  recorded 1933 (Clarence Ashley & Gwen Foster). Possible 17th-c. English roots.
- **PD rationale — FLAG (arrangement):** The **traditional melody and lyrics are
  PD** (no single author, predates copyright; 1925 publication is pre-1931).
  **BUT** many later *specific arrangements are separately copyrighted* (e.g. Alan
  Price/Animals 1964 arrangement; a 1937 Ludlow Music registration exists). Use the
  **traditional/Appalachian melody and changes**, not a modern recorded
  arrangement.
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_House_of_the_Rising_Sun
  - SecondHandSongs (traditional work entry): https://secondhandsongs.com/work/6485
- **Key / form:** Minor key, **6/8 (or slow 3/4) ballad**, typically 8-bar verse.
  Commonly in A minor.
- **Changes (A minor, the ubiquitous version):** Am – C – D – F / Am – C – E(7) /
  Am – C – D – F / Am – E7 – Am – (E7). Reharm value: strong minor-mode loop with a
  built-in major-IV (D) lift; loops beautifully and recolors easily.

## 7. Frankie and Johnny — traditional murder ballad

- **Origin / year:** Traditional American murder ballad (story tied to an 1899
  St. Louis shooting). The familiar version was set by the **Leighton Brothers &
  Ren Shields, c. 1908, pub. 1912**; first recording 1912 (Gene Greene).
- **PD rationale — FLAG (arrangement):** The **traditional tune is PD** (folk,
  predates copyright). The 1912 Leighton/Shields published version is also pre-1931
  → **US-PD**. Both the folk core and the 1912 printing are safe in the US; verify
  any *modern* folio arrangement separately.
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankie_and_Johnny_(song)
  - Traditional Tune Archive: https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Frankie_and_Johnny
  - Sheet Music Singer (PD setting): https://www.sheetmusicsinger.com/frankie-and-johnny/
- **Key / form:** Major key, **8-bar verse**, often a 16-bar AB strophe, moderate
  2/4 or 4/4. Commonly in C or G.
- **Changes (8-bar, in C):** C / C / C – C7 / F / F – F#dim / C – A7 / D7 – G7 /
  C – G7. (Ragtime-tinged turnaround with the F#dim passing chord.) Reharm value:
  the diminished passing chord and secondary dominants are already jazz-friendly;
  murder-ballad subject matter fits the game's noir.

## 8. When the Saints Go Marching In — traditional spiritual

- **Origin / year:** Traditional African-American spiritual; evolved early 1900s
  from several similarly-titled gospel songs. No single author.
- **PD rationale:** Traditional spiritual, predates copyright → **PD**. (Specific
  recorded arrangements, e.g. later big-band charts, may carry their own copyright;
  the tune itself is free.)
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_the_Saints_Go_Marching_In
  - Hymnary.org: https://hymnary.org/text/o_when_the_saints_go_marching_in
- **Key / form:** Major, **16-bar AABA-ish strophe** (4-bar phrases), bright 4/4
  (Dixieland two-beat). Commonly in C, F, or Bb.
- **Changes (16-bar, in C):** C / C / C / C7 / F / F / C / C / C / C / E7 / Am /
  F / C – G7 / C / (G7). Reharm value: textbook I–IV–V–vi with a secondary V/vi
  (E7→Am); the canonical Dixieland warhorse, trivially swung.

## Additional candidates (verified PD, lighter detail)

- **Careless Love — traditional blues/Dixieland.** 19th-c. ballad, PD as a
  traditional tune. **FLAG:** W. C. Handy's *"Loveless Love"* (1921) is his own
  copyrighted reworking — avoid that specific version; the folk "Careless Love" is
  PD. Major key, simple 16-bar I–V–IV–I sentimental loop.
  Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Careless_Love
- **Other clean Joplin rags** (all pre-1917, fully PD worldwide via IMSLP /
  Mutopia): *Elite Syncopations* (1902), *The Easy Winners* (1901),
  *Solace – A Mexican Serenade* (1909, habanera feel), *Bethena* (1905, a slow
  concert waltz — unusual 3/4 reharm fodder). IMSLP category:
  https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Joplin,_Scott

## Best repositories for original notation

- **IMSLP** (https://imslp.org) — original-edition scans; per-work copyright tags.
- **Mutopia Project** (https://www.mutopiaproject.org) — clean PD typesets
  (LilyPond), good for machine-readable extraction of the melodic skeleton.
- **Library of Congress** (https://www.loc.gov) — first-edition sheet-music scans.
- **Wikimedia Commons / Wikisource** — PD-tagged scans and transcriptions.
- **Internet Archive** (https://archive.org) — pre-1929 song-folio scans.
- **Traditional Tune Archive** (https://tunearch.org) — folk-tune ABC notation
  (machine-readable changes/melody for the traditional set).

## Summary of PD risk tiers

- **Tier A — clean, worldwide PD:** Maple Leaf Rag, The Entertainer (Joplin rags).
- **Tier B — US-PD, NOT EU/life+70 until 2029:** St. Louis Blues, Memphis Blues
  (Handy, d. 1958). Fine for a US project; flag if distributing in the EU.
- **Tier C — traditional tune PD, but avoid specific copyrighted arrangements:**
  St. James Infirmary (avoid the 1929 Mills/Primrose chart), House of the Rising
  Sun (avoid the 1964 Animals arrangement), Careless Love (avoid Handy's 1921
  "Loveless Love"), Frankie and Johnny, When the Saints Go Marching In.

The lowest-risk, highest-value feedstock for the reharmonizer: the **two Joplin
rags** (Tier A, machine-readable on Mutopia) plus the **traditional set's chord
skeletons** (Tier C — derive changes/melody, not a specific modern arrangement).
