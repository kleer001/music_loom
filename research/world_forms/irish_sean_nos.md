# Irish sean-nós singing

## 1. Tuning

Not surveyed.

## 2. Instrumentation

Unaccompanied solo voice, with a nasal tone quality and notably little vibrato or dynamic variation ([Sean-nós singing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean-n%C3%B3s_singing)).

The restraint is the point: with vibrato and dynamics held back, **ornamentation carries the entire expressive load**. That is an unusual allocation — most vocal traditions spread expression across several parameters at once.

## 3. Performance structure

Free rhythm, with the singer speeding up and slowing to suit the words. Long melodic phrases carry dense melismatic ornamentation.

Ornamentation comes in two kinds ([Ó Canainn / Project MUSE, *Melodic Ornamentation in the Connemara Sean-nós Singing of Joe Heaney*](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/169618)):

- **melismatic** — a note replaced by a group of adjacent notes.
- **intervallic** — additional notes filling the gap between two notes of the tune.

The generative rule worth carrying: **ornament placement is governed by text.** It follows syntax and poetic stress, and in Irish-language songs ornaments fall on *unstressed* syllables. Ornamentation is not sprinkled by density parameter; it is placed by a rule about the words.

Ornament density is also a regional marker — florid in Connacht, less decorated in Munster, comparatively stark in the north. One dimension, three settings, and the setting names the place.

Compare [`raga.md`](raga.md) §3, where gamaka is constitutive of the raga's identity, and [`shakuhachi_honkyoku.md`](shakuhachi_honkyoku.md) §3, where phrase length is set by breath rather than text. Three traditions of free-rhythm melody, three different things governing where the notes fall.
