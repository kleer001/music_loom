# Hawaiian oli and mele

## 1. Tuning

Not surveyed in cents. Melodic range in the chant styles is characteristically narrow, and pitch in several styles is not sustained at all — see §3, where the genre distinctions run along a delivery axis rather than a pitch one.

## 2. Instrumentation

**Oli** is unaccompanied voice. **Mele hula** adds the **ipu** (gourd drum) or **pahu** (sharkskin-headed drum) under a dancer, so the percussion is tied to movement rather than to the voice ([Kumukahi, *Oli*](https://kumukahi.org/units/na-kanaka/kaaike/oli); [Hula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula)).

**ʻIʻi** is the characteristic vocal device: a trill-like vibrato applied to held vowels, tapering the end of a phrase. It is a phrase-terminal ornament rather than a continuous one, which distinguishes it from Western vibrato — it marks where a line ends.

## 3. Performance structure

The genres are **differentiated by vocal technique**, not by pitch content or form ([Stillman, *Oli — Five Genres*](https://amykstillman.wordpress.com/2009/11/24/oli-five-genres/); [Things To Do Hawaii, *Protocols and Forms of Hawaiian Chant*](https://thingstodohawaii.com/hawaiian-culture/music/hawaii-chant/protocols-forms/)):

- **kepakepa** — rapid and rhythmic, **without sustained pitch**. Long texts such as prayers are delivered this way.
- **olioli** — sustained pitch with ʻiʻi tapering each phrase; the style closest to what a listener would call song.
- **hoʻāeae** — very melodic, short drawn-out vowels, heavy ʻiʻi.
- **hoʻouēuē**, **kāwele**, **ʻaihaʻa** — further styles on the same axis.

That taxonomy is the finding. Elsewhere in this survey a genre is named by its mode ([`ethiopian_qenet.md`](ethiopian_qenet.md) §3), its cycle ([`korean_sanjo.md`](korean_sanjo.md) §3), or its route ([`persian_dastgah.md`](persian_dastgah.md) §3). Here the same text can be delivered in several styles, and **the choice of delivery is the genre**. A synthesis parameter set — degree of pitch sustain, ornament density, vowel length — is closer to how these categories divide than a scale or a form would be.

The text is primary throughout; the chant carries genealogy, prayer and narrative, and the vocal setting serves it.
