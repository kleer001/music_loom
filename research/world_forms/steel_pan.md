# Steel pan

## 1. Tuning

Steel pan tuning is **additive synthesis performed with a hammer**, and one tuning source states it plainly: the steelpan is "essentially a mechanical means of implementing sound synthesis" ([Stockholm Steelband, *Acoustic Function of the Steel Pan*](https://stockholmsteelband.se/pan/tuning/theory19_acoustics.php)).

A note area is not tuned to a pitch; its **individual partials are tuned separately**. Standard practice tunes a second mode an octave above the fundamental and, where reachable, a third mode a twelfth or two octaves above. The ideal upper partial is a fifth above the octave, but it is often set to a third or fourth above instead because the fifth cannot always be reached while holding the fundamental at pitch ([Stockholm Steelband, *Tuning*](https://stockholmsteelband.se/pan/tuning/building09_tuning.php)).

This is the exact inverse of [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §2. Both are hammered metal idiophones; gamelan cultivates inharmonic partials as the sound, and the steel pan's whole craft is forcing partials **into** harmonic ratios so the note reads as pitched. Same material, opposite goals — which makes the pair a useful control on any claim that metal "wants" to be inharmonic.

The work is done entirely by ear.

## 2. Instrumentation

An oil drum with a sunk playing surface, note areas hammered into it, and a **skirt** — the cylindrical remainder of the drum — acting as a resonator.

The skirt is where a design decision becomes a compromise. Its length is set so its first resonance matches the lowest note on that drum, and a consequence is that the frequency response minimum then suppresses that note's **octave partial** — the very partial the tuner spent effort putting there. On bass pans the problem compounds, since the fifth above is usually a real note elsewhere on the playing surface.

A resonator tuned to help the fundamental fighting the partial structure of the same note is a coupled-system problem with no free answer, and it is worth knowing before modelling a pan as a bank of independent partials.
