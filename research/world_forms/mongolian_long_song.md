# Mongolian long song (urtiin duu)

## 1. Tuning

Not surveyed. The range is described consistently as extremely wide, reaching into falsetto.

## 2. Instrumentation

Voice, accompanied where accompanied at all by the **morin khuur** (horsehead fiddle) and **limbe** (side-blown flute) ([Long song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_song); [UNESCO / Atlas of Heritage, *Mongolian Long Song*](https://www.atlasofheritage.com/ich/mongolian-long-song/)).

The name is a common source of confusion: **"long" describes the duration of each note, not the length of the song.** A single syllable may be extended across many seconds, and the form's demand is breath control rather than stamina of memory.

## 3. Performance structure

Free compositional form, no fixed meter. The structural material is a **named vocabulary of vocal gestures**, which is what makes this tradition useful here — the ornaments are enumerated rather than left to taste:

- **shurankhai** — the prolonged tenuto note with deeply modulated vibrato on the vowel; the central device. Distinguished as **dan** (single) and **davkhar** (double).
- **nugalaa** — sharp notes modulated to a lower pitch.
- **usrelt** — tone leaps, upward and downward.
- **tsokhilgo** — modulated vocal pulsation.

An ornament taxonomy with names is a table a generator can hold. Compare [`irish_sean_nos.md`](irish_sean_nos.md) §3, which divides ornaments into melismatic and intervallic and places them by poetic stress; [`raga.md`](raga.md) §3, where gamaka is constitutive of the raga's identity; and [`hawaiian_oli.md`](hawaiian_oli.md) §2, where ʻiʻi marks the end of a phrase. Four traditions in this survey put ornament at the centre, and each specifies it differently: by type, by placement rule, by identity, by position.

Long song is the sustained-note extreme of that group. Where [`shakuhachi_honkyoku.md`](shakuhachi_honkyoku.md) §3 makes silence a first-class duration, long song makes the single held vowel one — and the ornament vocabulary is what happens inside it.
