# Andean siku (panpipes)

## 1. Tuning

Pentatonic and hexatonic collections are common, in flexible non-equal-tempered tunings.

The deliberate part: **corresponding pipes in the two halves of a siku pair are tuned roughly 10–30 cents apart**, so each note of the interlocked melody sounds as two slightly mismatched pitches beating at **2–5 Hz** ([Grokipedia, *Siku*](https://grokipedia.com/page/Siku_(instrument)); [worldinstruments.org, *Zampoña / Siku*](https://worldinstruments.org/instruments/zampona/)). **Confidence: moderate** — consistent across accessible sources, none of them a measurement study.

This is the same design decision as [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §2's ombak, reached independently on the other side of the world: a specified beat rate as a timbral goal.

## 2. Instrumentation

A single siku is split into two physical halves, **ira** (leader) and **arca** (follower), held by two players. Neither half can play a scale alone.

## 3. Performance structure

One melodic line, two performers. The ira holds one set of pipes, the arca the other, and the melody exists only in the interleaving — hocket as the *normal* mode of playing rather than a special effect. The practice embodies **yanantin**, the Andean principle of complementary duality: the instrument is built so that a melody requires two people.

Compare [`central_african_hocket.md`](central_african_hocket.md), where the same principle is taken to one pitch per player, and [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §3's imbal.
