# Persian dastgah and the radif

## 1. Tuning

The modern formalisation divides the octave into 24 quarter tones, devised by Ali-Naqi Vaziri (1886–1979), who introduced Western theory to Persian music and created the **koron** (half-flat) and **sori** (half-sharp) symbols to notate it.

The measured intervals do not sit on that grid. The [Ableton Tuning project's Persian radif section](https://tuning.ableton.com/persian-radif/dastgah-concept/), following Hormoz Farhat, gives:

| Interval | Cents |
|---|---|
| minor second | 90 |
| lesser neutral second | 135 |
| greater neutral second | 165 |
| major second | 204 |
| plus tone | 270 |

**Two distinct neutral seconds at 135 and 165 cents**, where a 24-quarter-tone grid offers one at 150. The grid is a notation, and the tradition it notates has a finer distinction than the notation can hold.

This is the third incompatible formalisation of the same region. [`maqam_arabic.md`](maqam_arabic.md) §1 uses 24 equal quarter tones; [`turkish_makam.md`](turkish_makam.md) §1 uses 53 Holdrian commas; this uses Vaziri's 24 with measured intervals that match neither. All three are modern impositions on older practice.

## 2. Instrumentation

Not surveyed. Tar, setar, santur, ney and kamancheh; the fretted lutes carry movable gut frets, which is the mechanism by which a fixed-pitch instrument stays flexible.

## 3. Performance structure

Farhat's position, quoted by the Ableton project, is the load-bearing one: **"there is no concept of scale in Iranian music."** What is usually called the scale functions as a palette of available pitches from which modes are drawn, and a mode typically uses four or five pitches, occasionally up to seven.

The **radif** is a repertoire of more than 250 melodic models called **gusheh**, organised into twelve **dastgāh** by modal relationship. *Dastgāh* means "the position of the hand" — the name is about the instrument, not about a pitch set.

A performance moves through an ordered sequence of gusheh, ascending through the registers, marked by:

- **shahed** — the central, most-emphasised tone of a gusheh.
- **ist** — the note a phrase comes to rest on.
- **forud** — the cadential gesture that returns to the dastgah's home region.

Each gusheh is a template for improvisation rather than a piece. The compositional unit is the model and the route through models — the same shape as [`maqam_arabic.md`](maqam_arabic.md) §3's ajnas and sayr, arrived at with a different vocabulary.
