## 3. Performance structure

**Aksak** is Turkish for "limping" ([Britannica, *Aksak*](https://www.britannica.com/art/aksak)). The cycle is built from durations of two categories, long and short, in a **3:2 ratio**. Bulgarian musicians count the short beat as 2 and the long as 3 — so the "unit" is the subdivision, and beats are unequal by design rather than by expressive delay ([Folkdance Footnotes, *Bulgarian Dance Rhythms*](https://folkdancefootnotes.org/dance/dance-information/bulgarian-dance-rhythms/)).

Common groupings:

| Meter | Grouping |
|---|---|
| 7/8 | 3+2+2 |
| 9/8 | 2+2+2+3 |
| 11/8 | 2+2+3+2+2 |

Bulgarian forms run to 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 22 and 25 beats per measure. Also called *neravnodelen razmer*, "unequally divided meter", and in the research literature **non-isochronous meter**.

The distinction that matters for a sequencer: this is not swing. Swing displaces a note within an even grid; aksak has **no even grid** — the beat itself alternates between two durations in a fixed ratio, and every subdivision inherits that. A 7/8 as 3+2+2 has three beats of unequal length, not seven equal ones grouped.

Timing studies find performed durations depart from the idealised 3:2 (*Timing Variations in Two Balkan Percussion Performances*, [Empirical Musicology Review](https://emusicology.org/article/4762/galley/5236/download/)). **Not read in full** — the abstract is the basis for this line.
