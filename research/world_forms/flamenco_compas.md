# Flamenco compás

## 1. Tuning

Not surveyed. The Phrygian-derived *modo flamenco* and its characteristic cadence are the melodic axis and are not covered here.

## 2. Instrumentation

Guitar, voice, and **palmas** (hand claps). The palmas are the relevant part structurally: they are clapped in groups of six against the twelve-beat cycle, generating counter-rhythms inside the compás rather than reinforcing it ([Tablao Flamenco 1911](https://tablaoflamenco1911.com/en/flamenco-compas-and-rhythm/)).

## 3. Performance structure

The **compás** of the soleá family is a twelve-beat cycle with accents unevenly placed. Internal accents, harmonic changes and closes fall at beats **3, 6, 8, 10 and 12** ([Grangala Flamenco](https://www.grangalaflamenco.com/en/blog/the-difference-between-the-solea-and-buleria-compas/); [Flamenco Wiki, *Compás*](https://flamenco.fandom.com/wiki/Comp%C3%A1s)).

Soleá and bulerías share that structure and differ in tempo — roughly 70–120 bpm against 220–240 bpm. **Sources disagree on bulerías' accent set**, some giving 1, 4, 8 and 9; recorded here as a disagreement rather than resolved.

Theory renders the cycle as an **amalgama**: a bar of 6/8 followed by a bar of 3/4 — hemiola, where the twelve pulses group as 3+3+2+2+2. The grouping is unequal and fixed, which puts flamenco in the same family as [`balkan_aksak.md`](balkan_aksak.md) §3, though flamenco's twelve pulses are even and only the *accents* are asymmetric, where aksak's beats are themselves of two lengths.

Worth noting without overclaiming: a twelve-pulse cycle with an asymmetric accent set is also what [`west_african_timelines.md`](west_african_timelines.md) §3 describes, and the accent positions fall in a comparable region of the same space. No lineage is claimed here; the resemblance is structural and the historical question is outside what was read.
