# Arabic maqam

## 1. Tuning

Theory divides the octave into 24 quarter tones, 50 cents apart ([Quarter tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_tone); [Arabic maqam, Microtonal Encyclopedia](https://microtonal.miraheze.org/wiki/Arabic_maqam)).

**Sources disagree on whether that is what is played.** 24-TET is widely described as a reference grid and notational standard rather than a performance rule. The neutral third is 350 cents in 24-TET but appears as 347, 355 or 359 cents in just-intonation accounts, and its performed pitch varies regionally — the Egyptian rendering is described as lower than the Syrian ([Neutral third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_third); [Offtonic Theory §7.9](https://offtonic.com/theory/book/7-9.html)). Treating 24-TET as the sound rather than the notation is the common simplification, and it is one.

Compare [`turkish_makam.md`](turkish_makam.md) §1: the same regional family, theorised on an incompatible grid.

## 2. Instrumentation

Not surveyed in depth. The fretless and flexible-pitch instruments central to the tradition — oud, nay, qanun with its per-course tuning levers, violin — are what make the pitch flexibility playable; a fixed keyboard cannot produce it. Recorded as a gap.

## 3. Performance structure

Maqamat are built from **ajnas** — cells of three to five consecutive notes with defined intervals, described as melodic atoms. A maqam is a lower jins with an upper jins stacked on it. The system recognises 72 heptatonic rows, of which perhaps 30 are in regular use and 8–10 form the everyday vocabulary ([Ethnic Musical](https://www.ethnicmusical.com/blog/maqam-music-for-beginners/); [Microtonal Encyclopedia](https://microtonal.miraheze.org/wiki/Arabic_maqam)).

**Sayr** is the conventional path a performance takes through the maqam — which regions are visited, in what order, where it settles. **Taqsim** is improvisation within that. The compositional unit is the cell and the route between cells, not the octave-scale.
