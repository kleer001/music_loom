# Ethiopian qenet (kiñit)

## 1. Tuning

Largely pentatonic, in many cases without semitones, and in **pure rather than equal temperament**, with flexible intonation in performance ([Qenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qenet); [Music of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ethiopia)).

The word **kiñit** names both the scale and, frequently, the tuning of the instrument playing it — because the krar, begena and masenqo are fretless or open-string instruments whose strings are retuned to the mode. Changing mode means retuning the instrument.

That is the same structural fact as [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §1's embat: **the scale is a property of a physical instrument, not a lookup table applied to one.** Two unconnected traditions where "what scale is this in" and "how is this instrument tuned" are the same question.

## 2. Instrumentation

**Krar** (lyre), **begena** (large lyre, its buzzing bridge giving it a sustained rasp), **masenqo** (single-string bowed lute). Not surveyed in depth; the begena's buzzing bridge invites comparison with the tanpura's javari in [`raga.md`](raga.md) §2 and was not investigated.

## 3. Performance structure

Four principal qenetoch — **tizita**, **bati**, **ambassel**, **anchihoye** — sometimes given as five with **yematibela**. Sources describe them as modal frameworks with distinct emotional associations rather than as scales alone, which places them alongside [`raga.md`](raga.md) §3 and [`persian_dastgah.md`](persian_dastgah.md) §3 in carrying affect and convention as part of the mode's identity.

**Confidence: low on detail.** A CNN-benchmark dataset paper on kiñit classification exists ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10118148/)) and was not read; it is the most likely accessible source of measured pitch data.
