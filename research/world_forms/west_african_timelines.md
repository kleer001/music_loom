# West African bell timelines

## 1. Tuning

Not a pitch tradition in the relevant sense; the timeline is carried by an unpitched or indefinitely-pitched bell.

## 2. Instrumentation

A metal bell carries the timeline and functions as the ensemble's reference — the part everyone else locates themselves against ([Ewe drumming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_drumming)). Its job is structural rather than colouristic.

## 3. Performance structure

The **standard pattern**, known internationally by its Cuban name **Bembé**, is seven strokes across twelve pulses ([Toussaint, *Classification and Phylogenetic Analysis of African Ternary Rhythm Timelines*, McGill](http://cgm.cs.mcgill.ca/~godfried/teaching/mir-reading-assignments/Classification-and-Phylogenetic-Analysis-of-African-Ternary-Rhythm-Timelines.pdf)):

```
[x . x . x x . x . x . x]      onsets at 0 2 4 5 7 9 11
interval-vector (2 2 1 2 2 2 1)
```

It is the bell pattern of Agbadza, an Ewe dance from Ghana and Togo.

Toussaint gives **computable reasons** why this pattern rather than another. Ten seven-stroke twelve-pulse bell patterns are in documented use. Simha Arom's **rhythmic oddity property** holds when no two onsets divide the full cycle into two equal halves; none of the ten has it outright, but the number of equal bi-partitions a pattern admits measures how close it comes. The Sorsonet group admits three, the Tonada group two, and **the Bembé group admits one — the minimum, so maximum rhythmic oddity**. A companion measure, **off-beatness**, counts onsets falling in positions no binary subdivision of the cycle reaches.

All ten patterns lie within four or five **swap** operations of one of two canonical patterns, where a swap exchanges an adjacent onset and rest.

That is a generative recipe with a stated selection criterion: enumerate seven-onset patterns in twelve pulses, score by equal bi-partitions and off-beatness, and the traditional repertoire is what scores well.
