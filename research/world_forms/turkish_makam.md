# Turkish makam

## 1. Tuning

The octave is divided into **53 equal commas** — the **Holdrian comma**, ~22.642 cents each, one step of 53-EDO. A whole tone is nine commas. The Arel-Ezgi-Uzdilek (AEU) system is a 24-note set constructed from Pythagorean commas, closely approximated by notes drawn from the 53-tone grid ([Turkish makam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_makam); [Akkoç, *Experiments on the Relationship Between Perde and Seyir in Turkish Makam Music*](https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/paperspdf/MP3204_02_Akkoc.pdf)).

The contrast with [`maqam_arabic.md`](maqam_arabic.md) §1 is the useful part. **Both traditions use about 24 practical pitches, theorised on incompatible grids**: Arabic theory divides the octave into 24 equal quarter tones (50 cents), Turkish conservatory training into 53 equal commas (~22.6 cents) of which 24 are used. They are not two notations for one system.

That two neighbouring traditions formalised the same practical vocabulary onto grids that do not align is itself the finding: the grid is a description imposed afterward, and neither grid is the sound.

## 2. Instrumentation

The **kanun** (qanun) carries **mandal** levers — small per-course latches that raise or lower a string by a comma at a time, reset mid-performance. Pitch flexibility is built into the instrument as a discrete, playable control rather than as a continuous bend ([Salamuzik, *Kanun (Qanun): History, Mandal System*](https://salamuzik.com/blogs/news/kanun-qanun-complete-guide-history-mandal-system-and-buying)). **Confidence: moderate** — vendor documentation, not an organological source.

## 3. Performance structure

**Seyir** is the conventional melodic path through a makam — where it starts, which regions it visits, where it settles. Two makams can share a pitch collection and differ entirely in seyir, which is why a makam is not recoverable from its scale.
