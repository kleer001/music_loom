# Gamelan (Javanese and Balinese)

## 1. Tuning

Two systems: **sléndro**, five tones roughly equidistant, and **pélog**, seven unequally spaced tones from which five-tone modes are drawn ([Slendro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slendro), [Pelog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelog)).

Neither is a fixed scale. Tuning varies island to island, village to village, and between one gamelan and the next; what stays consistent is the tuning *within a single set*, because instruments are tuned to each other rather than to a standard. A set's individual tuning character is **embat**, and it is valued rather than corrected.

Measured examples from the [Ableton Tuning project](https://tuning.ableton.com/sundanese-gamelan/intro-to-sundanese-gamelan/), which documents specific instruments rather than an idealised system: Gamelan Pelog Salendro TBN has its reference tone Tugu at 472 Hz; KF, a set built to the same principles, has Tugu at 451 Hz — about 79 cents apart. In Sundanese Degung tunings the 3–4 interval sits near 240 cents; in Madenda tunings the same interval sits near 80 cents.

The octave is stretched: the scale's periodicity is "usually slightly larger" than 2:1, reported around 1210 cents ([Slendro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slendro)). Octave stretching in Balinese gamelan has its own study (*Ombak and octave stretching in Balinese gamelan*, via Academia.edu). **Confidence: moderate** — the stretching is well attested, the specific figure rests on one encyclopaedia line.

## 2. Instrumentation

Bronze keys and gongs with **inharmonic partials**. Builders do not aim for a harmonic series, and the low bars carry between 16 and 26 prominent overtones each ([JASA, *Vibrational characteristics of Balinese gamelan metallophones*](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/127/5/EL197/783208/Vibrational-characteristics-of-Balinese-gamelan)). Pitch, clang, bloom and after-ring arrive together rather than as attack and decay of one tone.

**Ombak** ("wave") is the second half of the timbre. Instruments are built and tuned in pairs, one a few Hz above the other, and the ensemble's sound is the beating between them. The rate is deliberate and ensemble-specific: around 7 Hz for gong kebyar, around 5 Hz for gender wayang, 8–9 Hz for angklung ([Ombak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombak); [Smithsonian NMAA, *Lightbulb Ensemble*](https://asia.si.edu/research/essays/baumbusch/)).

The control here is a **beat rate**, not a detune spread.

## 3. Performance structure

**Colotomic**: structure is marked by *when the large gongs land*, not by bar lines. Gong, kenong and kempul punctuate a cycle perceived as circular rather than as a line with a start and end ([Gamelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamelan); [U-M CSEAS](https://lsa.umich.edu/content/dam/cseas-assets/cseas-documents/gamelan/JavaneseGamelanMusic.pdf)).

A skeletal melody, the **balungan**, is carried by saron and demung. Elaborating instruments figure over it, and **imbal** distributes a fast line between two players in alternation ([Imbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imbal)).

**Irama** is a level of *density* — elaborating notes per balungan beat — with five steps ([Irama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irama)):

| Level | Name | Density |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | irama lancar | 1:1 |
| I | irama tanggung | 2:1 |
| II | irama dados | 4:1 |
| III | irama wilet | 8:1 |
| IV | irama rangkep | 16:1 |

Tempo is a **separate** axis, **laya**, with three broad settings (seseg, sedeng, tamban) available at each irama level. The same irama can be played fast or slow. Density and tempo are two controls, not one.
