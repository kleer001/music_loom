# Carnatic tala

## 1. Tuning

Not covered here; see [`raga.md`](raga.md) §1 for the melodic side of the Indian classical traditions.

## 2. Instrumentation

Not surveyed. **konnakol**, the spoken-syllable recitation of rhythm, is worth noting as an instrument in its own right: the system is taught and performed vocally before it is played.

## 3. Performance structure

The **suladi sapta tala** system is a combinatorial generator rather than a list ([The scheme of 35 talas](https://carnaticmusicexams.in/2018/06/19/the-scheme-of-35-talas/); [Ramesh Shotham, *The South Indian Tala System*](https://shotham.org/globaltala-workshop/the-south-indian-tala-system/); [Morris, *Sets, Scales and Rhythmic Cycles*, Eastman](https://ecmc.rochester.edu/rdm/pdflib/talapaper.pdf)).

A tala is built from **angas** — sections with fixed or variable length:

- **anudrutam** — 1 akshara, fixed.
- **drutam** — 2 aksharas, fixed.
- **laghu** — variable, its length set by the tala's **jati** (5 options).

Seven tala families × five jatis = **35 talas**. Each beat may then be subdivided into 3, 4, 5, 7 or 9 equal parts — the **gati** or **nadai** — giving **175**. One cycle is an **avartana**, counted additively by section rather than by a repeating bar.

Two things follow. First, the cycle length is *derived* from a small specification rather than chosen: pick a family, a jati and a gati and the length falls out. Second, **gati is a density control separate from tempo** — the same as [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §3's irama, arrived at independently, and formalised as an enumerable dimension rather than a set of named levels.

The enumeration-with-a-selection-criterion shape is the same one Toussaint applies to bell patterns in [`west_african_timelines.md`](west_african_timelines.md) §3.
