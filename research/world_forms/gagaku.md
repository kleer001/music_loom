# Gagaku (Japanese court music)

## 1. Tuning

Two scale systems, **ryo** and **ritsu**, described respectively as brighter/major-leaning and more subdued/minor-leaning ([Organology, *Shō*](https://organology.net/instrument/sho/)). Not surveyed in cents.

## 2. Instrumentation

The **shō** is a mouth organ: seventeen bamboo pipes in a circular cluster over a lacquered wind chamber, each with a metal free reed, sounding on both inhale and exhale so a player can sustain indefinitely.

Its repertoire is **aitake** — eleven fixed tone clusters of five or six notes each, held and shifted slowly from one to the next. The clusters are dissonant by Western reckoning and are the harmonic field the melodic instruments (ryūteki flute, hichiriki reed pipe) move within ([Sonica Instruments, *Sho*](https://sonica.jp/instruments/en/product/sho/); [Shimizu, *Composing for Sho*](https://www.chatorishimizu.com/composingforsho)).

Two things a generator can use directly. First, **a fixed vocabulary of eleven clusters** is a chord table, but one indexed by cluster identity rather than by root and quality — no inversion, no voice-leading rules, just eleven shapes and transitions between them. Second, **continuous sustain**: the harmonic bed never articulates, which makes the melodic parts the only source of onset.

**netori** is a short prelude that establishes the mode before a piece.

## 3. Performance structure

Extremely slow tempo, with the shō's cluster changes as the slowest-moving layer. Not surveyed further; the layering of one very slow harmonic voice under faster melodic ones is the structural shape.
