# Korean sanjo and jangdan

## 1. Tuning

Not surveyed.

## 2. Instrumentation

A solo melodic instrument — gayageum, geomungo, daegeum, haegeum — with a **janggu** (hourglass drum, also spelled changgo) playing the jangdan. Two players, one of whom is the clock.

## 3. Performance structure

*Sanjo* means "scattered melodies". Its form is a **single monotonic sweep**, and the parameter being swept is cycle length ([Traditional Korean rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Korean_rhythm); [Grokipedia, *Sanjo*](https://grokipedia.com/page/sanjo_music)).

A **jangdan** is a rhythmic cycle, and the movements of a sanjo are named after the jangdan each uses:

| Jangdan | Beats | Character |
|---|---|---|
| jinyangjo | 24 | very slow |
| jungmori | 12 | moderate |
| jungjungmori | — | brisk |
| jajinmori | 4 | fast |
| hwimori | — | very fast |

**The longer the jangdan, the slower the tempo; the shorter, the faster.** Cycle length and tempo move together in the same direction, so the whole arc of a performance — often forty minutes or more — is one parameter shortening.

That is a complete long-form generator from a single monotonic control, and it is the opposite arrangement from [`gamelan.md`](gamelan.md) §3, where irama holds cycle length fixed and varies density *against* an independently-set tempo. Two traditions, two ways of splitting the same three quantities (cycle length, density, tempo) into dependent and independent variables.
