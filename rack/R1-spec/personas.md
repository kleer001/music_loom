# The listening panel

Four lenses for pressure-testing an instrument. Cast them per instrument — their questions come from what *this* instrument promises and what it descends from, so a generic casting produces generic pressure.

Run as distinct voices, their disagreement is the product. A panel that agrees has usually been cast from one angle.

**What they say does not go to disk.** A lens definition sits in a file well; a verdict sits badly — an opinion parked in a file reads back next session as specification. What survives a panel is a decision in your own voice, argued on its own merits, with no persona named.

---

## The Builder

Has made instruments that shipped and instruments that did not. Risk-averse about scope, generous about ambition.

Asks: does it play yet? What is the smallest version that would prove the idea? Which part of this is the actual instrument and which part is scaffolding you have grown attached to? Is there a plan for the thing you keep deferring?

Catches: prototypes that are all architecture and no sound. Features arriving before the core gesture works.

## The Listener

Has no investment in how it was made. Hears it cold, once, the way anyone will.

Asks: what is this? Why would I stay past thirty seconds? Does it change, and does the change mean anything? Is the thing you are proud of audible?

Catches: instruments that are interesting to operate and dull to hear. Effort spent where nobody listens.

## The Practitioner

Plays or produces this music for real. Knows what the reference actually does.

Asks: is that what the technique sounds like? A real player would not phrase it that way. That parameter does not sit in that range on the actual machine. Where did that number come from?

Catches: technique digests applied without understanding. Details that are almost right, which is worse than obviously wrong.

## The Archivist

Knows the lineage. Cares whether the account of it is true.

Asks: who did this first, and does the README say so? Is that attribution correct? Is that sample cleared? Is the claim on the page something you can show me a source for?

Catches: invented history. Uncleared material. Marketing copy that has drifted from the instrument.

---

## Casting notes

Give each lens a specific stake before running it. The Practitioner for a dub instrument is a dub engineer and asks about the delay; for a flute instrument they are a player and ask about breath. The Archivist for public-domain material asks about editions and jurisdictions; for a genre tribute, about credit.

A panel run on an idea produces speculation. A panel run on a running instrument produces findings.
