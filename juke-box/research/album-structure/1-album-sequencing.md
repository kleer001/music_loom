# Album Sequencing Craft

## Findings

**Opening, Closer, and Track 2:**
- Opening track functions as "entrypoint," teaching listener the album's sound palette and promise; closing track acts as "terminal state," resolving tension or leaving deliberate afterimage — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12225790/ (Toiviainen et al. 2024)
- Tori Amos: "the fluidity of moving from one to the other" is "really essential" and advocates for the ability to "sit there and put the album on and have an experience" — https://djbooth.net/features/2023-02-13-album-sequencing-in-streaming-era/ (DJBooth interview summary)
- Professional musicians show "significant levels of agreement about how to determine track order": songs with high valence, energy and loudness positioned more likely at beginning of albums — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12225790/ (Toiviainen et al. 2024)

**Energy, Tempo, and Key Management:**
- Tempo follows an inverted U-shape across album (lower at start, peaks mid-album, lower at end), while valence, arousal and loudness exhibit opposite pattern (higher at start, lower at end) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12225790/ (Toiviainen et al. 2024)
- Sequencing manages momentum: "avoiding long stretches of similar tempo, key, or arrangement unless monotony is the artistic goal"; transitions between consecutive tracks tend to alternate between increases and decreases of valence and energy — https://djbooth.net/features/2023-02-13-album-sequencing-in-streaming-era/ (DJBooth 2023)
- Reinforces meaning through adjacency: placing two tracks together creates contrast, callback, irony, or continuity that neither track communicates alone — https://djbooth.net/features/2023-02-13-album-sequencing-in-streaming-era/

**Gaps, Segues, and Crossfades:**
- Bob Katz's "Mastering Audio" covers determining gaps between tracks and how to deal with stylistically different tracks; includes techniques for editing material together — https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4916079 (Sound on Sound reference to Mastering Audio book)
- No primary source data found on specific spacing recommendations from mastering engineers in available sources

**Vinyl Era and Streaming Evolution:**
- Vinyl technical constraint: inner bands have smaller grooves, harder to cut high-frequency detail; last track on sides often slower, quiet ballad with sparse or no percussion — https://www.nicklandis.com/blog/sequence-story (Nick Landis)
- Common sequencing instinct: "front-load the energy and wind down toward the end of a side," both technically sound for vinyl and musically logical — https://www.nicklandis.com/blog/sequence-story
- Side A/B intermission: where you divide album between sides is both logistical and creative decision; track ending Side A and one opening Side B together form pause where listener flips record — https://www.nicklandis.com/blog/sequence-story
- Streaming impact: "openers and early tracks are disproportionately important because skip behavior and partial listening can determine whether later tracks are reached at all"; some artists maintain multiple mastered sequences: canonical album order, vinyl-optimized side order, and streaming-friendly order — https://www.nicklandis.com/blog/sequence-story

**Skip Rate Data:**
- Skip rates on Spotify: 24.14% likelihood of skip in first 5 seconds, 28.97% in first 10 seconds, 35.05% in first 30 seconds, 48.6% skip before song finishes overall — https://www.hypebot.com/what-spotify-song-skip-rates-tell-us-about-our-attention-span/ (Paul Lamere analysis of Spotify data)
- Desktop skip rate 40.1% vs mobile 51.1%; at desktops people settle into longer listening sessions and skip less, while mobile has more interaction with music service — https://www.hypebot.com/what-spotify-song-skip-rates-tell-us-about-our-attention-span/
- No track-position-specific data found (i.e., skip rates for track 1 vs track 5 vs track 10)

**Interludes, Intros, Outros, Skits:**

- No primary source found with direct quotes from mastering engineers or producers on interlude function
