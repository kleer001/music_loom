# Genre Cards: Pop and Groove Genres

## Mainstream Pop

### Album Norms
- **Track count**: 10-12 tracks was standard pre-streaming; modern streaming-era albums now feature 20+ tracks as "track creep" (more tracks = more plays for streaming revenue) — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)
- **Song length**: Median Hot 100 song fell from 4:10 (2000) to 3:30 (2018) to 3:07 (2021) to ~3:00 (2024); tracks under 3 minutes comprised 4% of Top 10 in 2016, jumped to 38% by 2022 — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)

### Song Form
- **Chorus placement**: First chorus arrives within 30 seconds, often within 15 seconds (vs. 45-75 seconds in CD era); corresponds to 30-second streaming threshold — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)
- **Intro length**: Compressed from 15-25 seconds to 0-5 seconds (80% decrease since 1980s) — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)
- **Bridge usage**: Declined from 75-85% in CD era to 15-25% now — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)
- **Outros**: Shifted from extended fades to hard stops or loops encouraging replay — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)
- **Key changes**: Dropped from ~30% in 1990s hits to nearly zero — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)

### Economic Driver
- A 2:15 track generates 26 payable streams/hour vs. 14 for 4:15 track (86% revenue difference) — https://weraveyou.com/2026/08/new-research-shows-real-reason-songs-are-getting-shorter/ (2026)

## Soul/Funk

### Song Form
- **Soul structure**: Verse-chorus form with vocal ad-libs intensifying through sections, building toward emotional climax (belted final chorus or extended vamp); call-and-response between lead and backing vocals (gospel inheritance) — *one source only*
- **Funk structure**: Riff-based, cyclical structures over conventional verse-chorus form; single groove rides for minutes, building through layered call-and-response, breakdowns, extended jams — *one source only*
- **BPM**: Funk 90–110 BPM (4/4 meter, strong slow groove); soul 60-130 BPM range — *one source only*
- **Vocal style**: Funk: short, repetitive phrases; soul: sustained notes and melisma carried through expressive line — *one source only*
- **Arrangement**: Drums and bass as foundation; electric guitar, keys, horns (saxes, trumpets), synths; Hammond B-3 organ for soul's warm bed; horn sections provide melodic hooks and rhythmic punctuation — *one source only*

## Reggaeton/Latin Urban

### Song Form
- **Dembow pattern**: Syncopated rhythm with kick driving the bar, snare/clap answering off-center; space between remains part of rhythm — *one source only*
- **BPM ranges by subgenre**:
  - Old School (1995–2006): 90–100 BPM
  - Reggaeton Pop (2017–Present): 90–105 BPM
  - Dominican Dembow (2010–Present): 115–130 BPM
  - Trap Latino (2016–Present): 130–160 BPM
  — *one source only*
- **Elements**: Coro (chorus) paired with dembow pulse, low bass entry or bright synth answer; classic dembow pattern, heavy perreo bass, brass stabs, crispy snare, catchy vocal-style synth melody — *one source only*

### Lyrical Themes & Language
- **History**: Originated in San Juan clubs (1991), blending dancehall, Latin rap, reggae with traditional Latin rhythms; emerged from Puerto Rican and Panamanian underground scenes — *one source only*
- **Language mixing**: Spanglish (Spanish-English blend) progressively became legitimate language; songs interchangeably rhyme both languages, no longer separate — *one source only*
- **Themes**: Reflect experiences, struggles, celebrations of everyday life; artists as storytellers, poets, cultural commentators using slang and cultural narratives — *one source only*
- **Jamaican influence**: Characteristic dembow rhythm borrowed directly from dancehall; early artists mixed Jamaican rhythms with local sounds — *one source only*

## Lo-fi/Chillhop

### Album Norms
- **Track length**: Most tracks 1-3 minutes — *one source only*
- **Album structure**: Designed for continuous looping; simple and repetitive, meant to loop smoothly and create vibe rather than progression-heavy experience — *one source only*

### Song Form
- **Meter**: 4/4 time like most popular music
- **Drum pattern**: Minimalist, typically 2-3 drum elements; kick on beats 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4; uncluttered patterns designed not to occupy listener attention
- **Overall structure**: Intro + main loop (with subtle building) + sometimes breakdown (elements drop out before returning) — *one source only*
- **BPM**: 60-90 BPM with space between drums, minimal groove; also cited 85-110 BPM with warm, nostalgic sound — *sources conflict slightly*
- **Production**: Layer 3-5 sounds including pitched samples, soft drums, ambient textures; use reverb, delay, chorus, bitcrushing generously for nostalgic effect; jazz samples and vinyl crackle characteristic — *one source only*

## Bossa Nova

### Song Form
- **Rhythm basis**: Based on samba with emphasis on second beat (often notated in 2/4); "swaying" feel vs. jazz "swinging"
- **Guitar pattern**: Thumb plays bass notes on 1 and 2; fingers pluck chords in unison on two eighth notes of beat one, then second sixteenth note of beat two; two-measure patterns with syncopation into measure two — *one source only*
- **Percussion**: Cabasa plays steady sixteenth-note pattern; bass emphasizes root and fifth on beats one and three — *one source only*
- **Form distinction**: Unlike 12-bar blues, bossa novas lack statement-repetition-resolution lyrical structure; greater harmonic and structural flexibility — *one source only*

### Vocal Style
- **Delivery**: Intimate and conversational; smooth, laid-back quality complementing sophisticated harmony — *one source only*

## Vocal Jazz

### Song Form
- **Standard form**: AABA (32-bar structure) = 4 sections × 8 measures each; A sections similar melodically but different lyrically; B section (bridge) musically and lyrically different — *one source only*
- **Performance format**: INTRO (optional) – FIRST CHORUS – SECOND CHORUS – SOLOS (optional) – FINAL CHORUS – ENDING (optional) — *one source only*
- **Historical source**: Great American Songbook standards stem from early-to-mid 20th century musical theater; written by Tin Pan Alley songwriters (Gershwin, Berlin, Carmichael, Kern, Mercer, Porter, Fields) — *one source only*

### Ensemble Format
- **Accompaniment**: Small jazz ensembles typical; piano, bass, drums, sometimes additional horns — *one source only*

## Ambient and Electronic Artist Albums

### Album Philosophy
- **Brian Eno approach**: Music can become "part of the ambience of the environment just as the colour of the light and the sound of rain" — *one source only*

### Album Structure & Design
- **Music for Airports**: Four compositions created by layering tape loops of differing lengths; designed to be continuously looped as sound installation (not traditional song-album) — *one source only*
- **On Land**: Mixture of synthesizer notes, nature/animal recordings, complex array of collected unused recordings; production involved "feeding unheard tape into mix, constant feeding and remixing, subtracting and 'composting'" — *one source only*

### Track Sequencing
- **Plateaux of Mirror approach**: Live improvisation method—Eno sets up sound, collaborator improvises to it, Eno occasionally adds elements; varied arrangement: tracks 2, 4, 7, 8, 10 mostly piano-oriented with minimal electronic processing; tracks 3, 9 feature light syncopation (chimes); track 5 piano backed with warm synthesizers — *one source only*

### Overall Approach
- Continuous/looping structure; minimalist compositions; innovative tape loops and synthesizer use; integration of field recordings and nature sounds; production as compositional tool
